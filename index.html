A chemist who learned to make data speak

From the catalysis lab to a university research office: real projects, real data, published and in use.

William Pinheiro holds a PhD in Chemistry from the Federal University of Amazonas (UFAM), including a sandwich doctorate at the University of Pisa, Italy. He spent years generating data at the bench — XRD, FTIR, HPLC, GC-MS, ICP-OES. That routine of spreadsheets, calibration curves and model fitting sparked his interest in the other half of the job: cleaning, modeling, explaining and communicating.

He is now a postdoctoral researcher at the Office of Research and Graduate Studies of Amazonas State University (PROPESP/UEA), where he builds the dashboards and data pipelines behind the university's internationalization — from scraping and APIs to semantic matching with language models. At the Amazon Bio-business Center (CBA), he developed semi-automated Power BI reports and Python data flows.

His specialty is chemistry; data analysis is the tool. The systems in this issue were built with generative AI assistance (Claude Code) for writing code: the questions, the choice of methods, the validation of results and the chemical interpretation are his own.

The common thread is the same as in experimental chemistry: methodological rigor and honesty about limitations. Small datasets are treated as small, correlation is not sold as causation, and every dashboard states what the data cannot support.

Cover story · Applied NLP

The algorithm that finds partners for the Amazon

Turning research lines into vectors to discover, among millions of publications, who in the world studies the same topics as UEA's graduate programs.

Before every international mission, UEA's research office faced a hard question: who is worth talking to? The answer came from manual searches, personal contacts and intuition. FAAP — Find a Academic Partner replaced that process with a reproducible pipeline.

The starting point is the official research lines of each graduate program. On the other side, the OpenAlex API returns researchers and publications from the target universities. Texts are translated from Portuguese to English, stripped of stopwords and lemmatized with spaCy.

The leap in quality came from replacing exact keyword counting with semantic similarity: a Sentence-BERT model (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) embeds each research line and each publication title, and cosine similarity ranks the candidates. Synonyms and equivalent technical terms are now recognized, and batch ranking processes an entire country in one run.

The same model classifies UEA's 166 research lines into up to three UN Sustainable Development Goals — making it possible to cross foreign universities and SDGs in a single matrix.

When chemistry calls for machine learning

A study on dye removal with Amazonian plant fibers became a modeling lab: explainable ML, response surfaces, nonlinear fitting and quantum chemistry — in a single dashboard.

Palm and pod fibers — untreated, acid-treated or base-treated — were tested as adsorbents for methylene blue dye. The data lived in a lab spreadsheet on Google Drive, so the first task was engineering: an ETL that pulls the latest version through the API, validates the structure of each tab and only then replaces the SQLite database. If the spreadsheet layout changes, the process fails loudly instead of silently writing bad data.

Validation exposed formula errors in the original adsorption capacity (Qₑ) column; values were recomputed from the standard equation.

On the 49 experimental runs, three algorithms — Random Forest, XGBoost and SVR — were tuned with GridSearchCV and 5-fold cross-validation. SVR performed best on the test set. SHAP values show adsorbent mass as the most influential variable, consistent with the physics of the process.

The dashboard also includes a simulator: users move the parameters and a Flask backend returns predictions in real time.

Rigor before R²

  • OFAT is not DoE. The data vary one factor at a time; RSM was applied retrospectively and the full 21-term model was singular. VIF-based selection kept the 13 terms with real support in the data.
  • Linearization misleads. On the same data block, linearized Langmuir gave R² = 0.03; direct nonlinear fitting gave R² = 0.87. The problem was the method, not the data.
  • An optimum is a hypothesis. The desirability optimum (differential evolution) is presented as a suggestion to be confirmed in the lab.

From shift-report PDFs to an incident map

Shift reports from the Amazonas State Fire Department became a dashboard with KPIs, a map and peak-hour patterns.

CBMAM incident dashboard. Incident type, battalion and shift are clickable and filter everything; Leaflet map colored by type; incidents per service day and a day × hour heatmap.

The source was PDFs exported from the dispatch system — one per shift, with summary tables and free-text blocks for each incident. The ETL uses pdftotext and regular expressions to extract status, address, coordinates, dispatched vehicles and outcome.

Open incidents reappear in the next shift's report; deduplication by ID prevents double counting. Burned area and liters of water have no dedicated field: they were extracted from the narrative text, with rules to discard implausible values.

The narratives contain victims' personal data. Hence privacy by design under Brazil's LGPD: PDFs never enter the repository and no personal data reaches the dashboard. The documentation also lists what the data cannot measure — such as response time — and what to request from the agency to measure it.

Three networks, fourteen universities, one dashboard

UEA's participation in the CAPES Global program — previously scattered across proposal annexes and spreadsheets — brought together in a single HTML file any manager can open in a browser.

Overview. The Conecta Mundi (UNESP), INTER-NÓS (UFMG) and SustentNET (UNICAMP) networks, with funding planned for UEA and programs per institution; each network has its own tab with a map and a thematic area × university matrix.

A Python script (build_dashboard.py) reads the macro-enabled workbook with openpyxl, extracts planned scholarship amounts from the budget PDF and cross-references everything with the committee registry in the research office's management system. The output is a single self-contained HTML file, with embedded logos and CSV export.

The “Explore programs” tab filters and sorts all 738 links from the annexes, and a committee × annex check automatically flags registry mismatches.

O algoritmo que encontra parceiros para a Amazônia

Como transformar linhas de pesquisa em vetores e descobrir, entre milhões de publicações, quem no mundo estuda o mesmo que os programas da UEA.

A Pró-Reitoria de Pesquisa da UEA precisava responder a uma pergunta difícil antes de cada missão internacional: com quem vale a pena conversar? A resposta vinha de buscas manuais, contatos pessoais e intuição. O FAAP — Find a Academic Partner substituiu esse processo por um pipeline reprodutível.

O ponto de partida são as linhas de pesquisa oficiais de cada Programa de Pós-Graduação. Do outro lado, a API do OpenAlex devolve pesquisadores e publicações das universidades-alvo. Os textos passam por tradução PT→EN, remoção de stopwords e lematização com spaCy.

O salto de qualidade veio ao trocar a contagem de palavras-chave idênticas por similaridade semântica: um modelo Sentence-BERT (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) converte linha de pesquisa e título de cada publicação em embeddings, e a similaridade de cosseno ranqueia os candidatos. Sinônimos e termos técnicos equivalentes passam a ser reconhecidos, e o ranking em lote roda um país inteiro de uma vez.

O mesmo modelo classifica as 166 linhas de pesquisa da UEA em até três Objetivos de Desenvolvimento Sustentável da ONU — o que permite cruzar universidades estrangeiras e ODS num único quadro.

Quando a química pede machine learning

Um estudo de remoção de corante por fibras vegetais da Amazônia virou um laboratório de modelagem: ML explicável, superfície de resposta, ajuste não linear e química quântica — em um só painel.

Fibras de palma e de vagem, tratadas com ácido, base ou em estado natural, foram testadas como adsorventes do corante azul de metileno. Os dados nasciam numa planilha de laboratório no Google Drive — e o primeiro trabalho foi de engenharia: um ETL que baixa a versão mais recente pela API, valida a estrutura de cada aba e só então substitui o banco SQLite. Se a planilha mudar de formato, o processo falha alto em vez de gravar dados errados.

A validação revelou erros de fórmula na capacidade de adsorção (Qₑ) da planilha original; os valores foram recalculados a partir da equação padrão.

Sobre as 49 corridas experimentais, três algoritmos — Random Forest, XGBoost e SVR — foram ajustados com GridSearchCV e validação cruzada k=5. O SVR teve o melhor desempenho em teste. Os valores SHAP mostram que a massa de adsorvente é a variável mais influente, coerente com a física do processo.

O painel inclui ainda um simulador: o usuário move os parâmetros e um backend Flask devolve a predição em tempo real.

Superfície de resposta. Modelo quadrático com termos escolhidos por VIF; R² = 0,959.
Pareto dos efeitos. ANOVA tipo II (statsmodels), α = 0,05.
Isotermas de equilíbrio. Langmuir, Freundlich, Temkin e Redlich-Peterson ajustados por regressão não linear, com resíduos e funções de erro χ², ARE, HYBRID e R²NL.
Cinética. Pseudo-2ª ordem com R²NL = 0,995 e análise de resíduos.
DFT B3LYP/6-31G* (pyscf + RDKit, estruturas via PubChem). O HOMO do alcóxido do tratamento básico sobe para −0,645 eV — uma explicação eletrônica para a maior adsorção observada.

Rigor antes do R²

  • OFAT não é DoE. Os dados variam um fator por vez; o RSM foi aplicado em retrospecto e o modelo completo de 21 termos era singular. A seleção por VIF manteve 13 termos com suporte real nos dados.
  • A linearização engana. No mesmo bloco de dados, Langmuir linearizado deu R² = 0,03; o ajuste não linear direto, R² = 0,87. O problema era o método, não o dado.
  • Ótimo é hipótese. O ponto da função desirability (evolução diferencial) aparece como sugestão para confirmar em laboratório.
